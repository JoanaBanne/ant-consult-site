The firm number comes out of the design, where the capital cost is traded off against what the system will cost you to run for the next twenty years.

As a rule of thumb, engineering services on an infrastructure project fall between 5 and 15% of total project capital cost. Where you land depends on the number of design variables and the level of reporting and drawing output you need, so we only price finally once we understand what you are trying to build. We charge in one of three ways.

Well-defined projects with few unknowns are a fixed-price fee based on complexity and the time required for the scope of work. Pipelines, pump stations and irrigation systems fall here, and ballpark costs for these project types are in our cost estimator.

Open-ended or not well-defined projects are charged on a time-and-cost framework, with hourly rates derived with reference to Engineering Council of South Africa (ECSA) norms. Large scale feasibility studies and flood protection works fall here, as does any project where it is not clear yet what the end result will look like.

Where ongoing services are required we will consider a retainer: a set monthly fee for up to a certain amount of non-transferable time, which makes cash flow predictable for both of us.

We carry full professional indemnity insurance, currently underwritten by Hollard, and we contract on the Consulting Engineers South Africa (CESA) short form of agreement.
