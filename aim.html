Our approach

The ANT Implementation Method (AIM)

A structured approach to getting projects right the first time

Every project has more than one right answer. AIM finds yours.

Agricultural water projects involve multiple stakeholders, competing priorities and a scope that keeps moving as the design develops. AIM brings the right people around the table at the right time, clarifies what the operation actually needs before the design is finalised, and delivers an outcome the full management team can stand behind.

How AIM works differently

Unlike traditional approaches that attempt to define every requirement before design begins, AIM uses an initial design as a practical tool for testing assumptions. We use that draft as the focus of a structured review session where the full client team, Projects, Operations, and management, pulls it apart together.

Identifying what does not work early is often the fastest way to define the right solution. That session gives us a clear, agreed brief that the final design is built from, eliminating the iteration cycles that come from trying to reverse-engineer requirements after the fact.

The review session

Three questions drive every AIM review

01
What works?

Identify what is aligned with your operation and objectives.

02
What does not work?

Pinpoint gaps, risks, and assumptions that could cause problems.

03
What would make this better for your operation?

Capture ideas and priorities that improve performance, efficiency and resilience.

Project deliverables
Design Drawings

Complete irrigation design drawings.

Bill of Quantities

Accurate BoQ for costing and procurement.

Design Report

Technical report and design justification.

Site Visits

2 site visits included in the design process.

Design Iterations

Three iterations: conceptual design, detailed design 1 and detailed design 2. Further iterations are billed at agreed hourly rates.

All deliverables are designed to support construction, operations and long term performance.

Typical design period
10 weeks

From information handover to final design package: concept design, the early review, two site visits and three design iterations. The clock starts once soil, water and survey data are in.

Recommended start
6–12 months

Before construction, to allow for design, contractor appointments and equipment lead times.
